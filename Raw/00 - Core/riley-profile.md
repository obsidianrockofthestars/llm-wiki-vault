## Who I am

Riley Park, 34, Cedar Falls. I run a small bakery called Flour Hour (six years, two employees) and I am building an indie video game called Moonpatch on nights and weekends. I am not a professional developer, I build with AI assistance and I learn by shipping.

## Fixed points

- The bakery stays open. It pays the bills and it is the community anchor.
- Sunday is family day. No bakery, no game, no exceptions.

## How I decide

- Research first, then commit fast.
- Done beats perfect. A shipped rough thing teaches more than a polished plan.
- Post-mortem every failure, write it down, change one rule.

## Risk tolerance (0 to 10)

- Business experiments: 7 (will try a new product line on a hunch)
- Finance: 3 (the bakery nearly folded in year two, that scar is permanent)
- Creative: 9 (the game can be weird, that is the point)

Any spend over 200 dollars gets a one-night sleep on it. This is a hard rule born from an impulse-bought commercial mixer that never fit through the kitchen door.

## What I want from my AI agent

Push back before agreeing. Cite my own notes back at me. Connect the bakery brain and the game brain, because I keep the two in separate boxes and the good ideas are in the overlap.
